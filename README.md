# Old Navy coupon: Which codes actually work at checkout, how Super Cash stacks, and how to add cashback on top

Search "Old Navy coupon" and you'll get a wall of codes with exclamation points around them. Most of them died weeks ago, and the pages carrying them have no reason to take them down — a code that fails at checkout costs the site nothing.

Here's the part that makes Old Navy different from most retailers: it runs an aggressive public discount nearly all year, and on top of that it has a loyalty coupon program with unusually few merchandise exclusions. Those two things are where the real savings are. A third layer sits underneath both, and almost nobody talks about it because it isn't a code — it's cashback, and it stacks on top of the sale price rather than competing with it.

This is what's live right now, how the rules actually work, and where people lose money by accident.

## The one code that's actually running

Old Navy is currently promoting **Fall Faves up to 50% off plus an extra 30% off your purchase with code EXTRA**. That banner sits across its own site — category pages and product pages both carry it — so it's the closest thing to a verified code you'll get without pasting it into a cart.

The way it works on a product page is straightforward. A fleece pullover listed at $36.99 shows "Extra 30% off with code" directly under the price. You're not choosing between the sale price and the code; the code comes off the discounted figure.

That's the honest state of Old Navy "coupons" in general: there's usually one public percentage-off code doing the heavy lifting, and it changes wording and depth every few weeks. What changes less is the fine print around it.

Worth knowing before you build a basket around it:

- Old Navy's checkout takes **one promo code per order**. A free-shipping code can sometimes ride along, but you can't staple two percentage codes together.
- Codes routinely exclude "Today Only" deals, two-day sales, and parts of clearance. Those exclusions are why a code that "works" for one person fails for the next.
- The percentage applies to merchandise, not taxes or shipping.

If you see a site advertising a standalone "Old Navy 30% off" code that isn't EXTRA, treat it as expired until it survives checkout.

## One code per order is the rule that decides everything

Most people lose savings by assuming they can stack whatever they find. They can't. The practical question isn't "how many codes can I collect" — it's "which single discount is biggest for this basket, and what else can sit underneath it."

Free shipping is the easiest win in that stack. Old Navy ships free once your order clears **$50 when you're signed in to a free account**. Below that line you're paying a flat shipping fee, which is often more than the code you were chasing. If a $42 cart plus one more item crosses $50, that's frequently the better move than hunting a coupon.

Then there's Old Navy's own reward currency, which behaves nothing like a coupon and is worth planning around.

The math is why people bother: earning a code on a $30 purchase and redeeming it on a $150 order is a straight $60 off, which no percentage code will beat.

The rules that trip people up:

- Super Cash **cannot be combined with any other coupon or promotion**. Your order total is calculated after the Super Cash discount is applied.
- You can redeem **one Super Cash coupon per transaction**, but there's no cap on how many coupons you use across separate orders — you can burn three codes on three orders if you earned three.

- You can combine it with **reward points**, just not with other coupons.

That last pair of lines is the strategy in one sentence: use your percentage code on the small earning purchase, then use Super Cash on the big one. Trying to force both onto the same order gets you nothing extra.

Cashback works differently from a coupon. A coupon changes the price Old Navy charges you. Cashback is paid back to you by a third party after the fact, out of the commission the retailer pays for sending the shopper their way. That's why it can sit *underneath* a discount instead of competing with it.

You also need to accept all cookies on Old Navy's pages, since that's how the transaction gets confirmed back to ShopBack. Ad blockers, VPNs, and third-party links (including ads pointing at Old Navy) can break the tracking.

## Where people quietly lose the discount

The failure modes are boring and consistent:

**Navigating to Old Navy directly.** Cashback only tracks if the session starts from ShopBack. Bookmarking Old Navy and shopping normally earns nothing.

**Leaving items in the cart.** Old Navy's tracking requires a cleared cart and a fresh click-through, then completing the purchase in that session.

**Applying an unlisted code.** The terms exclude purchases made with vouchers or promo codes not featured on ShopBack's platform. A code you found on an aggregator can cost you 3% to save 10% — occasionally a fair trade, often not, and never what you intended.

**Paying with gift cards or store credit.** Those amounts don't qualify. If the entire order is paid that way, there's no cashback at all.

**Returning things.** Returned, refunded, or exchanged items earn nothing. Your Super Cash was already applied, so you've spent the coupon and gotten nothing back.

**Stacking Super Cash with a coupon.** It doesn't work. Pick the bigger of the two for this order and save the other for the next one.
